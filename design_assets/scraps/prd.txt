1. BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE
Novaria currently lacks a single, authoritative system for registering citizens and managing
their identities throughout their lifetime. Birth records, naturalization records, identity
information and death records are maintained across different institutions. This makes it
difficult to establish and maintain one reliable identity for each citizen.
NovaID is a national identity and civil registration platform managed by the Novaria Identity
and Civil Registration Authority (NICRA). It will create and maintain a trusted identity record for
every eligible citizen and assign each citizen a unique Novaria Social Number (NSN).
The NSN will serve as the common identity reference across approved government services.
Systems such as OneHealth will verify a person's identity through NovaID without receiving
unrelated personal information.
NovaID will also serve as the public-facing identity platform through which citizens can access
approved digital government services. Where enabled, citizens will be able to select “Log in
with NovaID” on a connected government platform and authenticate without creating a
separate identity for each service. NICRA remains the government authority responsible for
regulating, operating and overseeing the NovaID platform.
This document uses three related names precisely, and keeps them distinct on purpose.
NICRA (Novaria Identity and Civil Registration Authority) is the government agency
responsible for the system: use it wherever the document is talking about who runs, approves
or governs something. NICRS (National Identity Civil Registration System) is the formal,
technical name of the system itself; it appears in this document's title and belongs in technical,
legal or architectural language. NovaID is the public-facing brand a citizen actually sees and
interacts with, such as on a login screen; use it for citizen-facing flows and everyday
references throughout the rest of this document.
The NSN is different again, and not a fourth name for the same thing: it is the 12-digit identifier
NovaID issues to each citizen, the way a passport office issues passport numbers rather than
being one itself. A citizen has an NSN; they log in with NovaID, not with an NSN.
NovaID will support the full identity lifecycle, including:
• Birth notification and registration.
• Registration of naturalized citizens.
• Issuance of an NSN.
• Identity verification.
• Identity updates and corrections.
• Periodic photo and biometric updates where applicable.
• Death notification and registration.
• Secure verification by approved government institutions.

• Maintain a complete audit history of identity-related actions.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
Identity information in Novaria is fragmented, inconsistent and difficult to verify across
institutions. Government agencies maintain separate records that are not always linked or
synchronized. This fragmentation creates several challenges:
• Citizens may have multiple or conflicting identities.
• Birth and death records may be incomplete.
• Identity issuance may be delayed because information cannot be verified quickly.
• Government institutions may struggle to confirm that an individual is who they claim to
be.
• Duplicate and fraudulent registrations may go undetected.
• Citizens may be required to submit the same information repeatedly to different
institutions.
• Government planning may rely on incomplete population information.
• Personal information may be shared excessively or without adequate controls.
• Citizens may have difficulty accessing essential services when their identities cannot be
verified.
• Changes made in one government system may not be reflected in other connected
systems.
NovaID will address these issues by establishing a centralized national identity registry,
standardized civil registration processes and controlled identity-verification services.
3. PRODUCT PRINCIPLES
3.1 One Person, One Identity
Each citizen will have one active NovaID identity record and one NSN. The NSN will not be
reassigned to another person.
3.2 Privacy by Design
Connected institutions will only receive the minimum information required for an approved
purpose.
3.3 Authoritative Identity Source
NovaID will be the official source of core citizen identity information. Connected systems may
maintain their own service-specific records but should use the NSN to identify the citizen.
3.4 Traceability
Every identity registration, search, verification, update, approval, rejection and exceptional
access must be recorded.

3.5 Human Oversight
High-risk registrations, suspected duplicates, material identity changes and disputed records

3.6 Inclusive Access
NovaID will support online and assisted registration. Controlled low-connectivity processes will

3.7 Separation of Government Records

Material changes to a citizen's identity must be supported by evidence, independently
approved and communicated to the citizen where appropriate.

5.2 Connected Institutions
OneHealth
OneHealth will submit birth notifications for births occurring in connected hospitals and health

Civil Registration Centres
• Register births.
• Process births that occurred outside health facilities.
• Process late birth registrations.
• Capture identity information and supporting evidence.
• Capture photographs and biometrics where applicable.
• Provide assisted services.
• Initiate approved identity updates.
• Submit applications to NICRA for review and approval.
Novaria Citizenship and Immigration Service
The Immigration authority will:
• Assess citizenship and naturalization applications.
• Approve or reject citizenship.
• Provide validated naturalization references to NovaID.
• Confirm the authenticity and status of naturalization approvals.
NovaID will not decide whether a person qualifies for citizenship. NICRA will create the identity
and issue an NSN after Immigration has approved citizenship and NovaID checks have been
completed.
Police Services
The Police system may use NovaID for lawful identity verification. Police access will be limited
by role, purpose and legal authority. Criminal investigations, arrests, warrants, convictions,

7. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
7.1 Birth Notification from OneHealth
For a birth occurring in a connected health facility, OneHealth must submit:
• Child's first name where already provided.
• Child's surname or proposed surname.
• Date and time of birth.
• Place of birth.
• Sex recorded at birth.
• Birth type, such as single, twin or triplet.
• Birth order for multiple births.
• Mother's name and NSN where available.
• Father's name and NSN where available.
• Guardian information where applicable.
• Attending healthcare professional.
• Health facility.
• Date and time of notification.
• Supporting birth record reference.
NovaID must:
• Validate that required fields are present.
• Check that the health facility and submitting officer are authorised.
• Generate a unique birth-notification reference.
• Check for a possible earlier notification of the same birth.
• Return the notification status to OneHealth.
• Place incomplete or conflicting notifications in an exception queue.
• Prevent the birth notification from automatically generating an NSN.
7.2 Birth Registration
A birth notification becomes a formal identity record only after civil registration is completed.
The Registration Officer must:
1. Retrieve the birth notification or create a registration for a birth without an electronic
notification.
2. Confirm the parent or guardian information.
3. Capture required residential and contact information.
4. Review supporting evidence.
5. Capture a child photograph where required by policy.
6. Submit the application for validation.
NovaID must:
• Check for possible duplicate births or identities.
• Validate parent or guardian NSNs where provided.
• Flag conflicting names, dates or parent information.

7.3 Birth Outside a Health Facility
Where a birth occurs outside a registered health facility, NovaID must allow an authorised

• Parent or guardian information.

NovaID must classify a registration as late when it is submitted more than 60 days after the
date of birth. Late registration must:
• Capture the reason for the delay.
• Require additional supporting evidence, such as a school enrolment record, a religious or
baptismal record, or a sworn affidavit of age declaration.
• Perform enhanced duplicate checks.
• Require approval by an authorised senior officer.
• Preserve all evidence and decisions.
• Allow the applicant to appeal a rejection.
7.5 Naturalized Citizen Registration

• Validate the naturalization approval reference.
• Confirm that the approval is active and has not been withdrawn.
• Capture the person's legal name.
• Capture previous names.
• Capture date and place of birth.
• Capture previous nationality where required.
• Capture contact and residential information.

• Check for an existing NSN or identity record.

7.8 Photo and Biometric Lifecycle
Biometric information forms part of the NovaID MVP because demographic information alone
may not be sufficient to prevent duplicate identities in a large population.
Biometrics Included in the MVP
• Facial photograph.
• Facial biometric template.
• Fingerprints for eligible citizens.
• Biometric exception records for citizens whose biometrics cannot be captured reliably.
Iris scanning will not form part of the MVP. It may be considered in a future version if required.
Biometric Requirements for Children
• A photograph may be captured at initial registration where required.
• The identity will initially be linked to the verified parent or guardian records and the
registered birth event.
• The child's photograph must be updated at age five.
• Fingerprints will be enrolled from age five where they can be captured reliably.
• Biometrics must be updated again at ages 12 and 18 as the child develops.
• NICRA must provide advance notification and a reasonable grace period for mandatory
updates.
The absence of usable fingerprints must not prevent an eligible child from receiving an NSN.
Failure to update a photograph must not automatically cancel citizenship or delete the NSN.
Biometric Requirements for Adults
Adults registering through naturalization, late registration or another approved enrolment
process must provide a current facial photograph, a facial biometric template, and available
fingerprints captured according to NICRA standards.
Adults must recapture their facial photograph, facial biometric template and available
fingerprints at intervals set by NICRA, nominally every ten years, or sooner if requested or if
existing biometrics can no longer be matched reliably.

7.9 NSN Generation and Management
The Novaria Social Number will use the following format:
• The NSN will contain 12 numeric digits.
• The first 11 digits will be generated randomly.
• The final digit will be a check digit used to detect common entry errors.
• The number will be displayed in groups of four for readability: XXXX-XXXX-XXXX.
• Dashes used for display will not form part of the stored NSN.
• The number must not contain embedded information about the citizen.
The NSN must not reveal:
• Date of birth.
• Sex.
• State or district.
• Registration centre.
• Citizenship type.
• Registration date.
The NSN-generation service must:
• Use a secure random-number generation process.
• Check the complete number against all existing NSNs before issuance.
• Apply the approved check-digit algorithm (Luhn / mod-10).
• Reject and regenerate any duplicate number.
• Record the date, time and system event responsible for generation.
• Prevent users from generating an NSN manually.
• Prevent an issued NSN from being changed or reused.
• Generate an NSN only after the registration has received final approval.
Where a citizen forgets their NSN, NovaID must retrieve the existing number after successful
identity verification; a replacement NSN must not be created. Where a physical or digital

7.10 Log in with NovaID: Digital Authentication Service
NovaID provides an authentication service known as “Log in with NovaID.” Approved
government platforms may use this service to authenticate citizens without creating separate
identity credentials for each platform.
The authentication process must:
1. Redirect the citizen from the connected service to NovaID.
2. Display the name of the requesting service.
3. Display the information the service is requesting.
4. Authenticate the citizen using an approved method.
5. Confirm that the requesting service is authorised.
6. Return an authentication result and only the approved identity attributes.
7. Record the authentication event in the audit log.
8. Return the citizen to the requesting service.
Connected services must not receive the citizen's NovaID password, PIN or biometric
information. Authentication methods may include:
• Password and one-time password.
• NovaID PIN.
• Approved authenticator application.
• Device-based authentication.
• Biometric authentication performed on the citizen's device.
The exact authentication methods included in the MVP will be defined during security design.

• NSN.

Search controls must include:
• Minimum search criteria.
• Role-based result limits.
• Masking of sensitive information.
• Rate limits.
• Prevention of unrestricted browsing.
• Logging of the search purpose.
• Monitoring of repeated searches for unrelated citizens.
• Additional approval for bulk or exceptional searches.

7.12 Identity Verification
Connected institutions may submit selected identity attributes for verification. NovaID may
return:
• Match.
• Partial match.
• No match.
• Record under review.
• Record restricted.
• Deceased status where the requesting institution is authorised to receive it.
• Service temporarily unavailable.

7.13 Identity Updates and Corrections
NovaID must distinguish between:
Minor Updates
Examples include: phone number, email address, residential address. Minor updates may be
completed after standard authentication and validation.
Material Identity Changes
Examples include: legal name, date of birth, place of birth, parentage, citizenship information,
sex recorded in the identity record, deceased status, and linking or merging identity records.
Material changes must:
• Require evidence appropriate to the specific change, for example a court order, marriage
certificate or government gazette notice for a legal name change, or medical or school
records plus a sworn affidavit for a date-of-birth correction.
• Be reviewed by a Verification Officer.
• Be approved by a separate Senior Approver.
• Preserve the previous value.
• Record the reason for the change.
• Record the initiating and approving officers.
• Notify the citizen where appropriate.
• Send approved updates to affected connected systems.
7.14 Duplicate Detection and Resolution
NovaID must screen every new registration against existing identity records before an NSN is
issued. Duplicate checks will use:
• Legal and previous names.
• Date and place of birth.
• Parent or guardian information.
• Contact information.
• Supporting-document references.
• Facial biometric matching.

Lawful residents and refugees who are not Novaria citizens are not issued an NSN. NICRA
issues these individuals a Novaria Resident Number (NRN): a 12-digit identifier in the same
XXXX-XXXX-XXXX display format as the NSN, generated by the same secure random-number
process and validated with the same check-digit algorithm, but held in a separate number
series so an NRN can never be mistaken for or reused as an NSN.
Connected systems must treat the NRN as a distinct identifier type, not a variant of the NSN,
and must record which type an individual holds. Where a resident is later granted citizenship
through naturalization, NICRA issues a new NSN and retires the NRN, preserving a record of
the change; the individual's prior identity record is linked but not deleted.

8. KEY USER JOURNEYS
8.1 Birth in a OneHealth-Connected Facility
1. The hospital records the birth in OneHealth.
2. OneHealth submits the birth notification to NovaID.
3. NovaID validates the hospital, officer and required information.
4. NovaID generates a birth-notification reference.
5. The parent or guardian completes civil registration at an approved centre or through an
approved process.
6. A Registration Officer reviews the notification and supporting evidence.
7. NovaID performs duplicate and consistency checks.
8. A Verification Officer reviews the application.
9. Exceptions are resolved or escalated.
10. The application is approved.
11. NovaID creates the identity record and generates an NSN.
12. OneHealth receives confirmation that the birth has been registered.
13. The parent or guardian receives confirmation.
8.2 Birth Outside a Health Facility
1. The parent or guardian visits an approved Registration Centre.
2. The Registration Officer records the birth details.
3. The required supporting evidence and witness information are captured.
4. NovaID checks for an earlier notification or registration.
5. The application is classified for enhanced review.
6. A Verification Officer reviews the evidence.
7. A Senior Approver approves or rejects the application.
8. NovaID creates the identity and issues an NSN after approval.
8.3 Naturalized Citizen Registration
1. Immigration approves the citizenship application.
2. Immigration sends or confirms the approval reference.
3. The citizen completes identity enrolment.
4. NovaID validates the Immigration approval.
5. NovaID performs demographic and biometric duplicate checks.
6. A Verification Officer reviews the application.

13.5 Offline-First and Low-Connectivity Registration
NovaID must support controlled registration in approved rural or low-connectivity centres.
Permitted Offline Activities
Authorised Registration Officers may: begin a new registration, capture demographic
information, capture supporting documents, capture photographs and biometrics, save an
encrypted registration package, and continue working on a previously saved local draft.
The following actions must not be completed offline:
• Final registration approval.
• NSN generation.
• Duplicate-resolution decisions.
• Material identity corrections.
• Death-registration approval.
• Institutional-access changes.
Local Storage Controls
• Be encrypted on the approved device.
• Be linked to the authenticated officer and registration centre.
• Be stored only on NICRA-approved devices.
• Receive a temporary, globally unique submission reference.
• Be protected from copying to unauthorised storage.

• Be deleted from the local device after confirmed synchronization and the applicable
retention period.
• Remain inaccessible after the officer's access is suspended.
Synchronization Process
When connectivity is restored:
1. The device authenticates with NovaID.
2. The queued registrations are uploaded securely.
3. NovaID validates the officer, device and centre.
4. NovaID checks whether each temporary submission reference has already been received.
5. Uploaded records undergo central validation and duplicate checks.
6. Successfully received records receive a central application reference.
7. The device receives confirmation of synchronization.
8. Confirmed local copies are securely removed according to NICRA policy.
Conflict Resolution
Where NovaID identifies conflicting information or another application for the same person: the
later submission must not overwrite the earlier record automatically; both submissions are
placed in an exception queue; the system shows the conflicting fields; a Verification Officer
reviews the records; supporting evidence may be requested; the approved record is retained
as the active application; and the rejected, merged or withdrawn submission remains in the
audit history.
Failed Synchronization
Where synchronization fails, the encrypted registration package remains in the local queue,
the system retries according to configured rules, the officer sees the failure reason and current
status, the same submission must not be recreated as a new application, and repeated failures
are escalated to technical support, which must not receive unrestricted access to the citizen's
readable information.
Offline Audit Requirements
The system must record: the officer who captured the information, the approved device used,
the registration centre, the date and time of initial capture, the date and time of
synchronization, offline edits made before synchronization, failed synchronization attempts,
and conflict-resolution decisions.

21. OPEN DECISIONS
NovaID as the product name, biometric scope for the MVP, and the general structure of the
NSN are already decided (Sections 1, 7.8 and 7.9). The administrative structure is resolved in
Section 20. The following items are now resolved:
1. Check-digit algorithm: the NSN's twelfth digit uses the Luhn (mod-10) algorithm.
2. Biometric vendor and matching technology: no specific commercial vendor is named in this
document; biometric templates and matching must conform to ISO/IEC 19794 (fingerprint)
and ISO/IEC 39794 (facial) interchange standards, with vendor selection made during
procurement.
3. Biometric matching thresholds: the three-tier confidence model (low, medium, high; see
Section 7.14) is decided. The specific numeric thresholds remain open and will be set
during biometric testing, since they depend on the vendor and data quality.
4. Biometric update intervals: children's biometrics are recaptured at ages 12 and 18 in
addition to the initial capture at age five (see Section 7.8). Adults recapture biometrics at
intervals set by NICRA, nominally every ten years, or sooner if requested.
5. Physical identity cards: not included in V1. V1 provides a virtual identity card through citizen
self-service (see Sections 7.16 and 17); physical cards are future scope.
6. Citizen self-service: included in V1 (see Sections 7.16 and 17).
7. Statutory registration period: a birth must be registered within 60 days of the date of birth;
a registration submitted after that period is treated as a late registration (see Section 7.4).
8. Evidence for births outside a health facility: a signed statement from the attending health
worker or traditional birth attendant, or, where none was present, a sworn affidavit,
together with witness or community verification where available (see Section 7.3).
9. Evidence for late registration: at least one corroborating document, such as a school
enrolment record, a religious or baptismal record, or a sworn affidavit of age declaration,
in addition to the standard registration evidence (see Section 7.4).
10. Evidence for material corrections: evidence appropriate to the specific change, for
example a court order, marriage certificate or government gazette notice for a legal name
change, or medical or school records plus a sworn affidavit for a date-of-birth correction,
reviewed case by case by a Verification Officer (see Section 7.13).
11. Identifier for lawful residents and refugees: a distinct identifier, the Novaria Resident
Number (NRN), not the NSN (see the new Section 7.20).
12. Governing legislation: the fictional Novaria Identity and Civil Registration Act (establishing
NICRA's mandate and the NSN) and the Novaria Data Protection Act (governing access
and misuse penalties); see Section 15.
13. Technology stack: a Next.js frontend with a Firebase/Firestore-style backend is the working
assumption for V1, consistent with OneHealth's precedent, and will be refined during
technical design (see Section 13.7). Development tooling: Google Antigravity is the
expected build environment; it does not change the stack itself.
14. Implementation timeline: a phased rollout, with Phase 1 covering core registration, NSN
issuance and OneHealth integration, and Phase 2 covering biometric expansion and
additional agency integrations; specific dates will be set during project planning.
